## 1. Laceration Repair Guide

### Body-region decision tree (region → material/size → technique → removal timing)

*(Cross-checked against AAFP's "Essentials of Skin Laceration Repair," Merck Manual Professional Edition, and StatPearls — general pattern confirmed, a few region-specific refinements below.)*

| Region | Material/Size | Preferred Technique | Removal | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scalp | 3-0/4-0 nonabsorbable (nylon/prolene) or staples; 3-0/4-0 absorbable for galea | Simple interrupted, staples, or hair-apposition technique | 7–10 days | **Blue-colored suture is specifically recommended for scalp** — a black/dark suture is hard to distinguish from hair, and blue improves visibility for removal. Hair apposition is a viable adhesive-free alternative in appropriate wounds. |
| Face (general) | 5-0/6-0 nonabsorbable, fine | Simple interrupted; tissue adhesive if low-tension, <2cm, dry edges | **Corrected: 3–5 days**, not a flat 5 | Cosmesis priority; low threshold for plastics referral. Consider removing every other suture at day 3 and the rest at day 5 to reduce cross-hatch scarring, per Merck Manual. |
| Eyelid/lid margin | 6-0/7-0 | Refer — do not repair lid margin or lacrimal system lacerations in the ED | n/a | Consult ophtho |
| Ear | 5-0/6-0 | Avoid cartilage-piercing sutures where possible; pressure dressing after | 5–7 days | Watch for auricular hematoma — drain to prevent cauliflower ear |
| Intraoral/lip | 4-0/5-0 absorbable (chromic/vicryl) for mucosa | Align vermillion border FIRST if lip involved, before any other suture | Absorbable — no removal | Vermillion malalignment is the most common cosmetic failure |
| Trunk | 3-0/4-0 nonabsorbable | Simple interrupted or running for long low-tension wounds | **Corrected: 6–10 days**, not 7–10 | Consider deep dermal absorbable layer if high tension. Staples are a good option here (speed/strength) but not on face/neck/hands/feet. |
| Extremities | 4-0/5-0 nonabsorbable | Simple interrupted or vertical mattress if eversion needed | **Corrected: 10–14 days confirmed by Merck Manual** | |
| Joints / high-tension areas | 3-0/4-0 + deep dermal absorbable layer | Vertical/horizontal mattress | 14 days | Splint in position of function if crossing a joint |
| Hands | 5-0 nonabsorbable | Simple interrupted | 10–14 days | **Exam tendon/digital nerve function before AND document after repair.** Staples not recommended here. |
| Feet/sole | 4-0/5-0 nonabsorbable | Simple interrupted | ~14 days (non-weight-bearing sole heals slower) | |

**New pearls found this pass, worth adding to the module:**
- **Pediatric patients get different material choices, not just different bedside manner:** absorbable sutures (fast-absorbing gut, vicryl rapide) are generally preferred over nonabsorbable in children specifically to avoid a second, distressing removal visit — face 5-0 fast-absorbing gut, trunk/extremities 4-0/5-0 vicryl rapide or plain gut, per a residency teaching source (Maimonides EM). Flag this as a pediatric-specific branch in the decision tree, not an afterthought.
- **Wound age cutoff for primary closure:** general teaching is <6–8 hours for most wounds, but the well-vascularized face and scalp extend that window to <12–24 hours — worth building as an explicit input (wound location + time since injury) rather than a single global cutoff.
- **Irrigation volume:** roughly 50–100mL of normal saline per cm of wound length (one UCSD standardized-procedure source cites 100–300mL as a typical total range) — worth including as a quick-reference number in the procedure card itself, not just technique description.

### Technique selection logic
- **Simple interrupted** — default for most wounds; allows individual adjustment
- **Vertical/horizontal mattress** — higher-tension wounds needing eversion
- **Running/continuous** — long, linear, low-tension wounds; faster closure
- **Subcuticular** — cosmetic, low-tension, no visible suture marks
- **Staples** — scalp, trunk, extremities; fast, less cosmetic
- **Tissue adhesive** — low-tension, non-mucosal, non-joint-crossing, <2cm, dry wound edges
- **Hair apposition** — scalp alternative to sutures

### Cross-links (don't duplicate — pull from Drug & Dosing Cards)
- Local anesthetic max dosing (lidocaine, bupivacaine ± epinephrine)
- Tetanus prophylaxis status check
- Antibiotic prophylaxis indications: bite wounds, joint-space involvement, gross contamination, immunocompromised host, delayed presentation (>18–24h)

## 2. Fracture / Splinting Guide

### Bone/region decision tree

| Region | Splint Type | Position | Key Neurovascular Check | Red Flags → Emergent Ortho |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Finger | Buddy tape or aluminum splint | Position of function | Digital cap refill, 2-point discrimination | Rotational deformity, open fracture |
| Hand/wrist | Sugar-tong or volar/dorsal splint | Wrist 20–30° extension, MCP 70–90° flexion, IP extension ("position of function") | Median/ulnar/radial nerve function | Compartment syndrome signs |
| Forearm | Sugar-tong splint | Elbow 90°, forearm neutral | Radial pulse, median/ulnar/radial nerve | Open fracture, neurovascular compromise |
| Elbow | Posterior long-arm splint | 90° flexion | Radial pulse, median/ulnar/radial nerve | Vascular injury (brachial artery) — high suspicion in supracondylar fractures |
| Humerus | Coaptation splint or sugar-tong | Arm adducted, elbow 90° | Radial nerve (wrist drop) | Radial nerve palsy — common with midshaft humerus fractures |
| Toe | Buddy tape | Neutral | Cap refill | Open fracture |
| Foot/ankle | Posterior splint ± stirrup | Neutral/90° | Dorsalis pedis/posterior tibial pulse | Compartment syndrome, dislocation |
| Tib-fib | Long leg posterior splint | Knee slight flexion, ankle neutral | Distal pulses, compartment check | Compartment syndrome — high index of suspicion |
| Knee | Knee immobilizer | Full extension or slight flexion | Popliteal pulse, peroneal nerve (foot drop) | Vascular injury with knee dislocation — even if reduced, needs vascular workup |
| Femur | Traction splint (prehospital/ED bridge) | Traction applied | Femoral/distal pulses | High-energy mechanism — assess for associated injuries |

### Universal splinting workflow
1. Assess and document neurovascular status BEFORE any manipulation
2. Adequate analgesia/procedural sedation as needed before reduction attempts
3. Apply splint with adequate padding, avoid circumferential casting acutely (swelling risk)
4. Re-check and re-document neurovascular status AFTER splinting
5. Discharge instructions: RICE, elevation above heart level, return precautions (numbness, worsening pain, color change, fever)
6. Ortho follow-up timing: typically 5–7 days for most closed fractures; sooner for intra-articular or displaced fractures

## 3. Nerve Block Guide

| Block | Indication | Approach | Typical LA | Key Complications |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Digital block | Finger/toe laceration repair, nail bed procedures | Transthecal or dorsal (web-space) approach | Plain LA (current evidence supports epi safety with normal digital perfusion, but plain remains most common in practice) | Digital ischemia (rare with epi), intraneural injection |
| Wrist block | Hand laceration/procedure anesthesia | Median, ulnar, radial nerve individually at wrist | Small volume per nerve | Intraneural injection |
| Hematoma block | Distal radius fracture reduction | Inject directly into fracture hematoma under sterile technique | Small volume plain LA | Infection if sterile technique breached |
| Facial nerve blocks (supraorbital/infraorbital/mental) | Facial laceration anesthesia without distorting wound edges | Landmark-based, at respective foramina | Small volume | Bruising, incomplete block |
| Intercostal nerve block | Rib fracture analgesia | Below rib at angle, ultrasound or landmark | Moderate volume per level | Pneumothorax, LAST (multiple levels = higher cumulative dose) |
| Femoral nerve block | Hip/femur fracture analgesia | Ultrasound-guided, lateral to femoral artery | Moderate-large volume | Intravascular injection, LAST |
| Fascia iliaca block | Hip fracture analgesia (landmark-friendly alternative to femoral) | **Confirmed technique — "two-pop" landmark method:** draw a line from ASIS to pubic tubercle, divide into thirds, puncture point is 2–3cm distal to the junction of the medial 2/3 and lateral 1/3. Feel for two losses of resistance (fascia lata, then fascia iliaca) | **Confirmed: 20–40mL** dilute local anesthetic (e.g., 0.2% ropivacaine) for an average adult, single fascial plane injection. **Peds: 0.8mL/kg**, diluted with saline to that volume | LAST |
| Popliteal/saphenous block | Lower leg/foot procedure or fracture analgesia | Ultrasound-guided posterior knee | Moderate volume | Nerve injury, LAST |
| Interscalene/supraclavicular/axillary (upper extremity) | Surgical anesthesia for shoulder/arm/hand procedures | Ultrasound-guided, primarily OR/anesthesia domain | Variable | Phrenic nerve paresis (interscalene), pneumothorax (supraclavicular), LAST |

**Cross-link, don't duplicate:** LA volume/concentration max dosing lives in Drug & Dosing Cards. LAST (Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity) recognition and lipid emulsion therapy protocol should be a shared reference callable from every block entry, not rewritten per block.

## 4. POCUS Guide

| Exam | Probe | Key Views | What You're Looking For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cardiac | Phased array (low freq) | Parasternal long/short, apical 4-chamber, subxiphoid | EF gestalt, pericardial effusion, RV strain/dilation, wall motion |
| Lung | Linear (superficial) or curvilinear | Multiple rib interspaces bilaterally | B-lines (pulmonary edema), lung sliding presence/absence (pneumothorax), pleural effusion |
| FAST | Curvilinear | RUQ (Morrison's pouch), LUQ (splenorenal), pelvis, subxiphoid cardiac | Free fluid, pericardial effusion; extended FAST adds lung views for pneumothorax |
| IVC | Curvilinear | Subxiphoid longitudinal | Respiratory variation for volume status/fluid responsiveness |
| Procedural guidance | Linear | Real-time visualization | Central line placement (IJ/subclavian/femoral), peripheral IV, arterial line, paracentesis, thoracentesis, LP assistance, nerve block guidance, foreign body localization |

## 5. Airway Management Flow *(new — orchestrates existing Calculators content)*

1. **Pre-intubation assessment** — pulls LEMON score, STOP-BANG (if elective/procedural context), ASA class from Section 2
2. **Pre-oxygenation** — NRB or NIV; consider apneic oxygenation via nasal cannula during induction
3. **Plan A** — direct or video laryngoscopy with standard induction agent/paralytic (pulls from Drug & Dosing Cards)
4. **Plan B** — alternate blade or video laryngoscopy device, bougie-assisted
5. **Plan C** — supraglottic airway (LMA) as rescue oxygenation
6. **Plan D** — surgical airway/cricothyrotomy for can't-intubate-can't-oxygenate scenario
7. **Post-intubation confirmation** — waveform capnography (gold standard), bilateral breath sounds, CXR for depth
8. **Documentation** — Cormack-Lehane grade obtained (pulls from Section 2), tube depth, cuff pressure

## 6. Procedural Sedation Workflow *(new — orchestrates existing Calculators content)*

1. **Pre-procedure** — informed consent, baseline vitals, ASA class + STOP-BANG screen (pulled from Calculators), NPO status consideration (often not a hard stop for emergent procedures — document risk/benefit discussion)
2. **During** — continuous pulse oximetry + capnography + BP monitoring; titrate to target depth (minimal/moderate/deep); airway equipment and reversal agents (naloxone, flumazenil) at bedside before starting
3. **Recovery** — Modified Aldrete score ≥9 (pulled from Calculators) before discharge; written discharge instructions; require a responsible adult escort
